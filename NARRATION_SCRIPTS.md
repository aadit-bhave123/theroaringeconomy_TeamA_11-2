# Student Narration Scripts

Each narration is designed to take roughly 30 to 40 seconds when read at a normal presentation pace. Record these with your own voices, then add the MP3s to `audio/`.

## Gallery 1: America's Postwar Economic Struggles

When World War I ended in 1918, Americans expected a return to peace and prosperity. However, the transition from wartime production created serious economic problems. Inflation raised the cost of everyday necessities, while returning soldiers competed for jobs. Businesses reduced wages and laid off workers, contributing to widespread strikes and labor unrest. The recession of 1920 to 1921 made these problems even worse. Although the United States had emerged from the war in a strong position, its economy first had to overcome a difficult period of adjustment.

## Gallery 2: The Economic Boom: Henry Ford and Mass Production

During the 1920s, American industries experienced remarkable growth, largely because of improvements in manufacturing and technology. Henry Ford helped transform automobile production by using the moving assembly line, which allowed factories to produce cars faster and at lower costs. As automobiles became more affordable, millions of Americans gained greater freedom to travel. The automobile industry also created demand for steel, rubber, glass, and oil. Ford's methods demonstrated how mass production could reshape the American economy and everyday life.

## Gallery 3: The Consumer Revolution and the Bull Market

The 1920s introduced a new era of American consumerism. Advertisements encouraged families to purchase automobiles, radios, and household appliances, while installment plans allowed people to buy products before paying their full price. At the same time, a booming stock market attracted investors hoping to become wealthy. Many purchased stocks using borrowed money, increasing financial risk. Although consumer spending and investing helped fuel economic growth, growing debt and speculation exposed weaknesses that contributed to the economic crisis at the end of the decade.

## Gallery 4: The Uneven Boom: Cities, Suburbs, and Rural America

American communities changed dramatically during the 1920s. For the first time, the 1920 Census recorded more Americans living in urban areas than rural areas. Cities offered industrial jobs, entertainment, and access to new technology. Meanwhile, affordable automobiles helped suburbs expand by allowing people to live farther from their workplaces. However, these changes were not experienced equally. Many rural communities lacked the electricity, transportation, and economic opportunities available in cities. The decade revealed a growing divide between different American lifestyles.

## Gallery 5: Two Economies: Urban Prosperity and Rural Hardship

Although the Roaring Twenties are remembered for economic prosperity, many American farmers experienced serious financial difficulties. During World War I, farmers increased production to meet high demand, often borrowing money to purchase land and equipment. After the war, demand weakened, crop prices fell, and many farmers struggled to repay their debts. While urban industries and consumer markets expanded, rural communities often faced declining incomes and limited opportunities. Their struggles reveal that the prosperity of the 1920s was far from universal.
